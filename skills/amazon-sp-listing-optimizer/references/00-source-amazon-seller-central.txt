The product title or product name (the Item name field on the List your products page) is the first piece of information that customers see when they search for a product on Amazon. Titles must clearly and concisely describe key product information. Clear titles help customers to identify, differentiate, and compare products, which simplifies their decision-making process and increases the likelihood of sale.

Product title requirements
Title requirements apply to all product types, except media product types, listed in all stores, except for Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Türkiye, and the United Arab Emirates. To list a product for the first time, your title must meet the requirements described below:

Requirement type	Details
Character limit	Titles must not exceed 75 characters, including spaces.
Promotional content	Titles must not contain promotional phrases, such as "free shipping" or "100% quality guaranteed."
Special characters	Titles must not use the following special characters: !, $, ?, _, {, }, ^, ¬, ¦. Other special characters, such as ~, #, <, >, and *, are allowed only in specific contexts. For example, you may use these symbols as product identifiers ("Style #4301") or measurements ("<10 lb"). Decorative usage of special characters is not allowed. For example, the title “Paradise Towel Wear Co. Beach Coverup << Size Kids XXS >>” is non-compliant because of the excessive use of symbols around the size.
Content requirements
Titles must contain the minimum amount of information that can be used to clearly describe the product, such as "Amazon Essentials Dress," "Columbia Hiking Boots," or "Sony Headphones."

Do not include restricted phrases, such as “FSA/HSA eligible.”

Word repetition	Titles must not contain the same word more than twice. For example, "Baby Boy Outfits Baby Boy fall Winter Clothes Baby Boy Long Sleeve Suspender Outfit Sets" is a non-compliant title. Prepositions, articles, and conjunctions may be used more than twice in a title. However, brand names are also subject to the two-instance limit. If part of a brand name appears in a different context (for example, "Old Navy and Navy Blue"), it is not considered a duplicate.
Note: If your product is already listed and the title doesn't comply with these requirements, the title might be automatically corrected or it might not appear in our search results. Policy-violating titles can be viewed directly in Manage All Inventory. Brand owners can also see these titles in Review listing changes.
Best practices
Creating high-quality product titles is important to help customers discover your products and can increase conversions. Customers quickly scan search results, so ensuring that your title captures the most important product information is key to making your listing discoverable. Additionally, long or cluttered titles can be hard to read. The ideal title should concisely provide key information about the physical product, without excessive detail or non-essential information.

If the title length is insufficient to include all details of your product, a new field called Item highlights has been introduced that you can use to add additional descriptive information (refer to the FAQ below for more details).

To provide the best possible customer experience, follow these guidelines:

Guideline	Compliant example	Non-compliant example	Explanation

Frequently asked questions
What is the difference between product titles and product names on Amazon stores?

Product titles and product names are the same thing on Amazon stores.

How can I add more descriptive information about my product if the required title length is only 75 characters?

When you update your title to the 75-character limit, you will be able to add item highlights. Item highlights provide an additional 125 characters for product details like materials or recommended use cases. These should be written as comma-separated phrases, instead of full sentences.

For example: Instead of a long title such as “Fast Charger Adapter, PPS Supported, Compact Charger for models like MacBook Air/Macbook Pro/iPhone 14/iPhone 13/Galaxy S22/iPad Pro/Pixel and More, Cable Not Included”, add the key product details such “USB-C, PPS Support, Cable not included” to the item highlights for a better customer viewing experience.

How will item highlights be visible to customers along with titles?

Item highlights will be visible below titles in search results and on product detail pages.

How do I handle brand names that contain special characters?

Brand names containing prohibited characters (such as !, {, or }) should be entered in the "Brand name" field. Special characters in the brand field are exempt from title requirements and may appear in the brand byline on detail pages and search results.

What should I do if my product doesn't have a brand name?

For products without a brand name, enter "generic" in the "Brand name" field. Do not enter a brand name in the title.

How do I edit the product title if I disagree with the title suggested by Amazon?

You can make changes to your titles and Item highlights at any time in Manage All Inventory.

Product bullet point requirements
Bullet points are concise statements that highlight key product features and benefits in order to help customers make informed purchasing decisions. Well-crafted bullet points can increase product discoverability in search results and can improve conversion rates by clearly communicating essential product information. Bullet points help customers quickly understand if the product is right for them.

Bullet point guidelines
Writing guidelines for high-quality bullet points:

Component	Best practices
Capitalization	Begin with a capital letter.
Bullet point count	Include at least three bullet points.
Character length	10-255 characters per bullet point.
Formatting	Format as a sentence fragment, and don't use end punctuation.
Use semicolons to separate phrases within a single bullet point.
Numbers	Write numbers one to nine in full, excluding names, model numbers, and measurements.
Measurements	Include a space between digit and unit (example: "60 ml").
Structure	Add a header with a colon followed by a description (example: "Cotton fabric: Made from 100% cotton").
Content focus	Highlight product features, benefits, and how the product meets customer needs. Avoid brand marketing stories.
Content consistency	Maintain data consistency across product variants. Minimize duplication with other product attributes such as title, product description, or product overview.
Claims	Avoid subjective, performance, or comparative claims unless they’re verifiable on packaging. Avoid unsubstantiated award claims or consumer survey results.
References	Do not divert or refer to other products not included under the ASIN. Do not compare your product to competitor brands.
Examples of high-quality bullet points:

Cotton fabric: Made from 100% cotton for softness and breathability
Long sleeve: Long sleeves add coverage and style
Loose fit: Relaxed fit allows for easy movement and comfort
Machine washable: Durable construction allows for easy care
Versatile style: Perfect for dance practice, playtime, or outdoor activity

Attributes guide
Product attributes are specific facts unique to your product. This page explains why they're important for your listings, and how to prioritize them effectively. Understanding and implementing comprehensive product attributes can help increase your product visibility in search results and provides customers with the essential information they need to make confident purchasing decisions.

Product attributes vary based on the product type you select, such as the following examples:

Shoes have attributes like color and size.
Lamps have attributes like wattage and bulb type.
Some attributes combine multiple values to provide complete information. For example, unit count consists of both a number value and a unit count type (such as "12 count" or "6 pack").

Benefits of complete attributes
Improved search visibility

Product attributes help index your products, which ensures they appear in relevant customer searches. They also power the refinement options (the filter check boxes in the left panel) that helps customers narrow their search results.

To learn more, go to Keyword attributes explained.

Enhanced product evaluation

When customers visit your product detail page, comprehensive attributes help them understand your product specifications and determine if it meets their needs. The more complete your product attributes, the higher the chance customers have all the information needed to make a buying decision.

AI assistant support

Complete product attributes enable Rufus (Amazon's AI shopping assistant) to provide more accurate context to customers while they search for products or evaluate specific items.

Attribute categories and priorities

Attributes in listings are categorized into three views:

Required: Must be completed for your listing to be created
Recommended: Important attributes that customers typically look for
All: Complete set of available attributes
We continuously curate these attribute lists based on the latest customer trends. We recommend that you provide all attributes in the Recommended view to ensure your products include the information customers typically look for during search and evaluation.

To stay updated with the latest attribute requirements, review the planned changes document.

AI-generated people disclosure
Some jurisdictions may require disclosure when videos contain photorealistic AI-generated people. If your video includes photorealistic AI-generated people, you must tag that video with the appropriate metadata before uploading it to Amazon.

How to add the required metadata

Use an IPTC-compatible metadata editor to add the keyword contains-synthetic-performer to the dc:subject (XMP) field of your video file.

If you include this metadata, Amazon will add a disclosure to inform audiences that the video includes AI-generated people, where applicable.

For more information, go to How to tag media that contains an AI-generated person.

You also have the option to check “AI-generated people” for AI-generated people within your image when you upload your image to your Creative Assets within A+ Content Manager and we will apply the necessary metadata for you.

When to provide this metadata

Provide the contains-synthetic-performer tag if your video features people who were entirely generated by AI (not based on a real person).

When not to provide this metadata

Do not provide this metadata if any of the following conditions apply:

Your video only features real people (even if they have been altered with AI-powered tools) or
Your video only features characters from movies, video games, or other expressive works (or example, the poster art of a movie or a video game cover art)
Your video does not feature any people
The people in your video aren’t photorealistic

Search optimization
Customers must find your products to be able to buy them. This guide explains how to accurately classify and list your products to optimize them for search.

Customers who shop on Amazon use keywords or browse refinements to find what they're looking for. By providing relevant and complete information for your product, you may increase your product's discoverability, which could lead to increased sales, so it’s important that your products are classified accurately in Browse. Product information like title, brand, product description, and other attributes must all be accurate and compliant with detail page rules. Any products that don’t comply with our guidelines may be suppressed from search until the quality issues are resolved.

Note: We reserve the right to not use all supplied content from the product description, including bullet points, for retrieving products in customers' shopping search results. Reasons for not using all keywords may include, but are not limited to, search computational efficiency, potential manipulation of shopping results, irrelevant search terms, and offensive or illegal terms. However, it's recommended to keep these details updated as per the style guidelines.
Search and browse
Customers can discover products by browsing or searching. Most often customers use a combination of browse and search.

Browse

To help customers find products easily, Amazon developed a detailed product hierarchy or browse-tree structure. Customers refine by category and subcategory links until they reach the most specific product type. Amazon uses Item Type Keywords to classify products under the correct browse nodes.

Accurate product classification optimizes discoverability, making it easier for Amazon to help customers find what they're looking for through browsing and searching. Classifying products is like placing your products in the correct aisle and on the correct shelf in the store. When customers search in All Departments, Amazon may limit the shopping results to products that match specific category classifications or product descriptors.

Selecting and applying browse keywords

Start with the item to classify and refer to Classify your products using Browse Tree Guides - BTG to learn about Browse Tree Guides. Browse Tree Guides are category-specific documents that provide specific keywords for your products.

Note: The item type keyword must have the same spelling and formatting as listed in the Browse Tree Guides. Make sure to select keywords at the most specific subcategory available.
Example: If you're selling women's running shoes:

Too broad: "Shoes" or "Athletic shoes"
Correct: "Women's road running shoes"
Search and generic keywords

For customers to find your products on Amazon, it's important to provide search terms that they might use when searching for what they want to buy. Search terms are keywords that are used by the search engine for lexical matching between an ASIN and a query. For example, if you’re listing a stainless steel refrigerator, you could include terms like fridge, freezer, icebox, cooler, energy-efficient, frost-free, adjustable shelves, temperature control, stainless steel, kitchen, household, French door, and ice maker.

It's important that you provide honest details about your product, including compatibility for electronics or functionality for appliances.

You can add search terms for your products. These search terms should only include generic words that enhance the discoverability of your product. For example, if you're selling headphones, your search terms can contain synonyms such as "earphones" and "earbuds." These are not visible on product detail pages, and can be updated in the Generic Keyword field of your listing.

For more information on Search, visit Use search terms effectively.

Note: We limit the length of the Generic Keyword attribute to less than 250 bytes. Search terms can be eliminated if prohibited search terms such as brand names, ASINs, or offensive language are supplied as generic keywords.
Search terms best practices
Below are some best practices to optimize your listings for search:

Maintain the text length limit of search terms
Make use of synonyms and avoid repeating keywords
Use spelling variations, and avoid common misspellings
Include abbreviations
Use all lowercase letters
Separate words with spaces, and avoid using punctuation marks such as semicolons, colons, and dashes
Use either singular or plural words, don’t use both singular and plural forms of the same keyword
Don’t use brand names, ASINs, profanity, or offensive terms
Avoid using articles, prepositions, and other short words like “a”, “and,” “or,” “the,” or “with”
Don’t use temporary statements like “new” or “on sale now,” or subjective claims like “best” or “cheapest”
Note: Violating these rules may result in your ASIN being suppressed and may put the health of your selling account at risk.

Understanding search behavior
Search query automatic correction

An ASIN may sometimes be unsearchable due to an automatic correction of the search query. When a search query gets corrected, the customer will see a Showing Results message with the corrected query at the top of the results. There will also be a Search instead for [original query] link for cases where the spell-corrected query isn't helpful for the customer and they want to view the results for their original query.

ASIN position fluctuation

We don’t guarantee a specific placement for an ASIN within search results. ASIN positions can fluctuate as we continuously strive to present the most relevant results to customers.

If the position of an ASIN is changing, it may be due to the location of their search, the type of account they are logged in to, or whether they’re on desktop or mobile. You may not see the ASIN in the same position as reported by the Search Troubleshooter tool. This is expected behavior.

Search result limitations

Amazon limits the number of ASINs shown on the search results pages and does not show results after a certain number of pages, depending on the type of search and number of results per page.

If the ASIN doesn’t appear within the set limit of results, it won’t be possible to navigate to it via the search query using the page numbers or next page links at the bottom of the search result page. Customers can use a more specific query or use filters to refine the results to discover the ASIN in the search.

Search Troubleshooting tool
The goal of search troubleshooting is to help you understand your product discoverability issues. If manual search troubleshooting tips haven't helped diagnose the search issues, you can use a tool called Determine why an ASIN is not searchable.

Note: Access this tool by searching for ‘ASIN is unsearchable' within Seller Central help. Input the Search URL, ASIN (child/non-parent ASIN), and the tool will diagnose why the ASIN is not searchable.
If you need additional support on this topic, try using Amazon's Seller Assistant.
